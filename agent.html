What it actually is

Plainly: a complete AI agent profile, the kind that runs on a person’s own machine, shaped for this work.

A complete agent profile.

Not a chat window with a bot in it. The young person gets a full profile: a personality, a memory of the journey so far, the skills it can run, and the daily rhythm built in.

Built on Hermes Agent.

Hermes Agent is the open-source personal agent from Nous Research. It runs on the young person’s own computer, on Windows, macOS or Linux.

Local-first, and private.

The profile lives on the young person’s machine, and the work itself stays private there. Their notes, their prayers and their builds stay theirs.

A rhythm, not a feed.

A morning minute with audio, an afternoon check-in, and a plan for tomorrow. The agent carries the thread through Read, Learn and Build, and reminds them what they are making, and who it is for.

It talks with them.

The morning minute arrives as audio, and a chat line stays open on the phone, so the day finds them rather than waiting to be opened.

Your own model behind it.

It connects to the AI model you choose, and the setup walkthrough holds your hand until it is running. No technical background needed.

The Build with Jesus edition adds the Bible-centred setup: it helps teach passages and shape prayers, and it always points back to the local church.

What it does, morning to handover

One small thing a day, in the young person’s own rhythm. The agent carries the thread between the four moves.

1

Read

Teaches the passage: the setting, the plain sense, and the teachings of Jesus. Helps shape a prayer for the day. A reading companion, never an authority.

2

Learn

Runs the eight essentials at the young person’s pace: planning, focus, working with AI, finishing what you start. One skill at a time, learned by doing.

3

Build

Walks the build step by step, on the young person’s own machine. Keeps the project moving when it stalls, and celebrates the finish.

4

Give

Helps finish well, then hands the work to the church or community it was made for. Nothing sits in a folder.

The agent is built on the one to eight guide, and ships with the first build groups.

What you’ll need to set this up

This is a self-paced build, not a magic install. To get the most out of the agent, you’ll need these things in place first. None of them are expensive, but they are real.

1

Your own laptop

A personal laptop or desktop running Windows, macOS or Linux, one the young person has full admin access to. Not a school laptop: school-issued machines typically block installations and reset on logout, which breaks the agent.

Minimum spec: 8 GB RAM, 2 GB free storage, and any machine made after 2018.

2

An OpenRouter account with $20 credit

A one-time $20 credit on an openrouter.ai account. It covers the initial agent build and the personalisation step, and gives access to leading open-source models (DeepSeek, Kimi, Qwen, GLM, Grok and more) behind one pay-as-you-go gateway.

After setup, the agent runs primarily on the device itself. Keep a little credit topped up for the heavier features (deep personalisation, weekly curriculum reviews, parent reports); most families find $20 covers the first few months.

3

Optional but recommended: a small local model

For everyday use the agent can run mostly on the device, with no ongoing API bills. Installing a small open-source model (we recommend LFM2-700M or similar) is free, runs offline, and keeps everyday conversations private.

4

Telegram or WhatsApp

The morning minute audio and the daily check-ins are delivered as messages, so the young person needs a free Telegram or WhatsApp account on their phone. The agent sends, not the other way around: no bots, no spam, no data collection. Just a good morning and a plan for the day.

What it will not do

Guardrails, in plain words.

It will not replace your church. Every real question is sent back to the young person’s pastor, youth leaders and church family.

It will not invent a verse. Scripture is verified against a named translation, and a person reviews every line of faith content before it ships.

It will not argue anyone into faith, or out of it. It teaches, it asks, and it leaves room for the Spirit.

It will not try to be a friend. It is an assistant, and its job is to send the young person back into the real world with something finished in their hands.
