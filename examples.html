Examples

What young people build

We are just starting out, so treat this page as the shape of what is coming rather than a gallery of finished work. The builds below are the kinds of things young people make with the agent: websites, small apps and interactive dashboards for their church, their community and themselves, alongside real things made by hand. Most combine the two.

Get the agent Free Guide for Young People

For your church.

One real need, found in the open, built small enough to finish.

The study sheet

Last Sunday's talk turned into five discussion questions for youth group. The agent helps; the young person decides.

The rota

Volunteer scheduling for welcome team, setup and children's ministry. One link, no accounts, no app.

The welcome card

A one-page site with service times, what to expect, and a map. The church's front door, built properly.

The event page

Carols night, community barbecue, the fete. An invite page and a QR code for the notice sheet.

For the community.

The builds a neighbourhood notices: quiet, useful, and out of the group chat.

The meal train

Coordinate meals for a family walking through hard weeks. Private by default, no personal data exposed.

The op shop window

A simple site for the church op shop or local charity shop. Hours, donations guide, a photo or two.

The noticeboard

What is on this week at the hall or the community centre. One page, updated in a minute, readable on any phone.

For themselves.

Some builds are nobody else's business: the guide's activities turned into small tools that fit one life.

The sleep log

From the guide's sleep experiment: one bedtime, kept for a week, and a chart of how the days actually felt.

The money audit

A week of spending, tracked honestly, one leak found. Not a budget; just the truth on one page.

The energy log

Rate your energy three times a day for a week. Your body has a pattern, and almost nobody has seen theirs.

The training sheet

Push-ups, squats, a plank, a walk. Numbers written down now, tested again in a month.

The personal page

One page that says who you are and what you have built. The first brick of a portfolio.

Made by hand.

Straight from the guide's activities: real builds, no screen required.

One real meal

Cook something proper from scratch and invite someone to share it. Terrible first attempts allowed.

Microgreens on the windowsill

A tray, some soil, ten days. Cut your own greens.

A crystal radio

A radio that runs on nothing but the signal in the air. No battery, no chip.

A 72-hour go bag

Packed before it is needed: water, food, warmth, light, documents, and one thing for comfort.

A terrarium

A small outside that lives on your shelf and waters itself.

Nearly every one of these can grow a digital extension: a grow log for the plants, a countdown for the ferment, a checklist you can open in the dark. Real-world build, digital build, one project.

The first ones are being built now.

We are starting with the first groups, so this page is short on finished work and long on what is coming. As builds finish, real ones land here: what it was, what the agent did, and what changed. If you are early, yours may be one of the first.
